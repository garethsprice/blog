# TTNW Pre-Draft Subset

Seven tests from the TTNW 14-test rubric that can be meaningfully applied to a brief before drafting. Score as pass / flag / fail.

## F1 · Thesis Architecture (Tier 1)
Can you state the thesis in a single sentence specific enough to be disagreed with? Does every planned section serve it? "AI will transform healthcare" fails. "The bottleneck in healthcare AI isn't the model, it's the schema" passes.

## F3 · Mechanism Before Interpretation (Tier 2)
Does the brief separate what happened from what the author thinks about it? At brief stage: does the angle promise to explain a mechanism, or just state an opinion?

## Fx1 · Intellectual Honesty (Tier 2)
Can you name a specific person or institution that would disagree with the thesis? Can you state their strongest argument in terms they would endorse? If you cannot name the person, the debate may not exist.

## Fx3 · Stands-Alone Self-Sufficiency (Tier 3)
Will the piece need to define insider terms for the named audience? Flag any terms that require introduction. A cold reader with no prior relationship to the author must be able to follow.

## O1 · Originality of Thesis (Tier 1)
Has this argument been made before? Search for the thesis online. If many pieces make substantially the same claim, ask: what does the author know from direct experience that those writers don't? If O1 fails, kill or fundamentally reframe — do not proceed to drafting.

## E3 · Analytical Depth (Tier 2)
Does the brief promise a "why" or "how" insight — a mechanism, a second-order effect, a non-obvious connection? Or is it planning to describe at the surface level? The paragraph that answers "why" is usually where the real insight lives.

## E4 · Contextual Richness (Tier 3)
Does the brief reference specific data, incidents, or precedents? Can the argument be situated in a broader landscape — historical precedent, industry dynamics, intellectual tradition?
