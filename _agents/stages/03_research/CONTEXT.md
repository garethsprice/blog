# Stage 03: Research

## Purpose
Gather evidence, data, sources, and examples the draft will need. Structured raw material with attribution — not a draft, not analysis.

## Inputs
| File | Layer | Purpose |
|------|-------|---------|
| `../01_op_ed_selection/output/[topic-slug]-brief.md` | 4 (working) | Angle and key points to research |
| `../02_pre_draft_ttnw/output/[topic-slug]-ttnw-pre.md` | 4 (working) | Flagged gaps and risks to address |
| `stages/03_research/references/source-standards.md` | 3 (reference) | Source quality tiers and citation rules |

## Process
For each key point in the brief:
1. Find supporting evidence (production data, published research, named companies)
2. Find counterevidence (what complicates or challenges the thesis)
3. Collect specific numbers, dates, names the draft can use
4. Note source quality tier (see source-standards.md)

Structure output as:
- Claim → evidence → source → tier
- For every person and organisation the draft may name: a one-line identification for first mention ("Ata-Malik Juvaini, a Persian historian who served the Mongols")
- For every source: the full citation details APA needs (author, date, title, publisher or outlet, URL), and the exact wording of anything that may be quoted
- Counterarguments found → evidence → source
- Specific quotable data points
- Gaps: claims that couldn't be substantiated (flag for human)

**Do not fabricate sources.** If evidence doesn't exist, say so. Mark uncertain claims with `[UNVERIFIED]`.

## Outputs
- `[topic-slug]-research.md` → `output/`

## Quality Checks (human review gate)
- Are sources real and verifiable?
- Enough Tier 1-2 to ground the piece?
- Did research surface a better angle? Note it.
- Are gaps acceptable or thesis-undermining?
