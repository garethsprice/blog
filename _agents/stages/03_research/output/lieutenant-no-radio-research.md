## A. Ancient and medieval

### Roman organisation (mechanism 6)
- Mid-Republic (Polybius' era): a legion was 4,200 men (5,000 in emergencies) (Polybius 6.20). Hastati, principes and triarii were each split "into ten companies" (maniples), with "two centurions and two optiones" per maniple (6.24). So a century was about 60 men, or 30 among the triarii. Polybius Book 6 never uses the word "cohort". Tier 2. https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Polybius/6*.html
- Polybius 11.23 (206 BC) calls a three-maniple grouping a "cohort": it existed around 200 BC as an ad hoc unit. Battle unconfirmed (Ilipa vs Baecula).
- 8-man tent group and 80-man century: Imperial period, Pseudo-Hyginus, *De munitionibus castrorum* 1: "One tent… shelters eight men. A complete century has 80 soldiers". Tier 2.
- Correction: Vegetius 2.13 gives 10 men per tent and centuries of 100. It does not support 8/80.
- Safe phrasing: "By the Imperial era: eight to a tent, 80 to a century, six centuries to a cohort."

### Tessera watchword (mechanism 5 / cadence)
- Polybius 6.34 (Paton): a man "attends every day at sunset at the tent of the tribune, and receiving from him the watchword — that is a wooden tablet with the word inscribed on it". He passes it "before witnesses" from maniple to maniple, and it must return "to the tribunes before dark". "If any one of them is missing, he makes inquiry at once, as he knows by the marks from what quarter the tablet has not returned". Tier 2.
- 6.35–37: cavalry go the rounds with written orders and collect a tessera from each post found awake. They bring "back the tesserae at daybreak to the tribune". A missing tessera identifies the failed post, which is tried by court-martial.
- Correction: "tesserarius" is Vegetius' term (2.7). Polybius says only "a man is chosen".

### Cannae, 216 BC (failure: shared authority)
- Polybius 3.110: "It is the custom, when the two Consuls are present, that they should take the chief command on alternate days". There was "a dispute and misunderstanding between the two leaders". Varro advanced "in spite of the protests and active opposition of his colleague". 3.113: Varro commanded on the day of battle.
- Livy 22.45.5: Varro "hung out the signal, without saying a word of the matter to his colleague" (Foster, Loeb; Latin confirmed, English from search snippet).
- Caveat: O'Connell, *The Ghosts of Cannae* (2010), ch. 7: Paullus "was the grandfather of the historian's patron, Scipio Aemilianus". Varro was "Cannae's designated scapegoat". So Polybius' blame on Varro is suspect, but the alternation rule itself is attested by both Polybius and Livy.

### Mongols (mechanisms 4, 6)
- Juvaini, *History of the World-Conqueror* (tr. Boyle, 1958), vol. I p. 31: people were divided "into companies of ten, appointing one of the ten to be the commander of the nine others", and likewise by hundred, thousand and tümen. Tier 2. https://archive.org/details/historyoftheworl011691mbp
- Hunt as rehearsal, Juvaini pp. 27–29: Chinggis held "that the hunting of wild beasts was a proper occupation for the commanders of armies". If game escapes the ring, "the commanders of thousands, hundreds and tens are clubbed therefor". War "is after the same fashion, and indeed analogous in every detail". Nerge = the hunting line.
- Feigned retreat: use Kalka 1223 (about nine days of retreat, per Britannica, from a search snippet only). Avoid Mohi. Legnica rests on Długosz, writing about 200 years later.

### Span of control
- US Army FM 6-0 (May 2014) para D-7: "As a rule, commanders can effectively command two to six subordinate units." Tier 2. https://www.milsci.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/sitefiles/fm6_0.pdf (check whether the 2022 edition keeps the line)
- Hamilton, *The Soul and Body of an Army* (1922) p. 229: "groups of three" near the top, "groups of six" at the foot (via Nickols 2011).
