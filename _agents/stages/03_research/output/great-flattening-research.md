## 1. David Bowie — The "Running Water" Prediction

### The 2002 NYT Interview

**Source:** "David Bowie, 21st-Century Entrepreneur" by **Jon Pareles** (not Neil Strauss — common misattribution), *The New York Times*, June 9, 2002, Arts & Leisure section, p. 30.

**Exact quotes:**

- **"Music itself is going to become like running water or electricity."**
- **"The absolute transformation of everything that we ever thought about music will take place within 10 years, and nothing is going to be able to stop it."**
- **"You'd better be prepared for doing a lot of touring because that's really the only unique situation that's going to be left."**

Bowie also predicted copyright would cease to exist within a decade and that live performances would become artists' primary revenue source as recorded music was commodified like a utility.

### The 1999 BBC Newsnight Interview

**Source:** BBC Newsnight, broadcast **December 3, 1999**. Interviewer: Jeremy Paxman.

**Exact quotes:**

- **"I don't think we've even seen the tip of the iceberg. I think the potential of what the Internet is going to do to society, both good and bad, is unimaginable. I think we're actually on the cusp of something exhilarating and terrifying."**
- When Paxman dismissively said "It's just a tool though isn't it?", Bowie replied: **"No. It's an alien life form."** (laughing) **"Is there life on Mars? Yes, it's just landed here."**
- **"I'm talking about the actual context and the state of content is going to be so different to anything that we can really envisage at the moment, where the interplay between the user and the provider will be so in simpatico it's going to crush our ideas of what mediums are all about."**

Video verified by Snopes as authentic; widely available via BBC Archive.

## 2. Marshall McLuhan — "The Medium Is the Message"

### Core Thesis

**Source:** *Understanding Media: The Extensions of Man* (1964), McGraw-Hill. Page numbers reference the 1964 first edition.

**The medium is the message (p. 7):**

> "The medium is the message. This is merely to say that the personal and social consequences of any medium — that is, of any extension of ourselves — result from the new scale that is introduced into our affairs by each extension of ourselves, or by any new technology."

**Content is always another medium (p. 8):**

> "The 'content' of any medium is always another medium."

He illustrates: the content of writing is speech, the content of print is the written word, the content of the telegraph is print.

### What Print Did to Thought

**Source:** *Understanding Media*, pp. 14-16; also *The Gutenberg Galaxy: The Making of Typographic Man* (1962).

- **"Print created individualism and nationalism in the sixteenth century."** (*Understanding Media*, p. 16)
- Typography creates **"uniformity, continuity, and linearity"** (*Understanding Media*, pp. 14-15)
- Print culture favored linear, sequential thought; detachment; specialization; privacy; the separation of senses.

### What TV/Electronic Media Did (Mosaic/Simultaneous)

**Source:** *Understanding Media*, Chapter 31 ("Television") and passim.

- **"Electric implosion now brings oral and tribal ear-culture to the literate West."** (p. 50)
- **"Stepping-up of speed from mechanical to instant electric form reverses explosion into implosion."** (p. 35)
- McLuhan described the TV image as a "mosaic mesh" requiring active viewer participation — fundamentally different from the high-definition, passive reception of print. Electronic media create "presentness, juxtapositions, closeness" and what he called "mosaic" thinking.

### Extensions, Amputations, and Numbness

**Source:** *Understanding Media*, Chapter 4 ("The Gadget Lover: Narcissus as Narcosis"), pp. 41-47.

- **"Any invention or technology is an extension or self-amputation of our physical bodies."** (p. 41)
- **"With the arrival of electric technology, man extended, or set outside himself, a live model of the central nervous system itself. To the degree that this is so, it is a development that suggests a desperate and suicidal autoamputation."** (p. 43)

Each new extension produces a "narcosis" or numbness as a protective response. The Narcissus myth is central — Narcissus was numbed by his own extended image, unable to recognize it as himself.

**Note on autonomous extensions:** [UNVERIFIED] McLuhan does not explicitly discuss extensions becoming autonomous/self-directing. His framework is about extensions producing numbness — the danger is that we fail to perceive what our extensions are doing to us, not that they gain agency. This is an important distinction for the essay.

### The "Global Village" — Warning, Not Celebration

This is the most commonly misunderstood McLuhan concept. He did not mean utopian harmony.

**Source:** *The Gutenberg Galaxy* (1962), p. 31:

> "The new electronic interdependence recreates the world in the image of a global village."

**Source:** Interview with Gerald Stearn, in *McLuhan: Hot & Cool* (1967), p. 272:

> **"The global village absolutely ensures maximal disagreement on all points."**
> **"It never occurred to me that uniformity and tranquility were the properties of the global village."**

**Source:** *Understanding Me: Lectures & Interviews* (2003), p. 265:

> **"The global village is a place of very arduous interfaces and very abrasive situations."**

McLuhan later replaced "global village" with **"global theater"** (c. 1970) to emphasize the performative, participatory dimension. His book title *War and Peace in the Global Village* (1968) is itself a signal — "war" comes first.

## 3. Baudrillard's Four Stages of Simulation

**Source:** Jean Baudrillard, *Simulacra and Simulation* (French original: Editions Galilee, 1981; English translation: Sheila Faria Glaser, University of Michigan Press, 1994).

The key passage is from the opening essay, "The Precession of Simulacra."

### The Four Successive Phases of the Image

Exact quote (English translation, p. 6):

> "These would be the successive phases of the image:
>
> 1. It is the reflection of a basic reality.
> 2. It masks and perverts a basic reality.
> 3. It masks the absence of a basic reality.
> 4. It bears no relation to any reality whatever: it is its own pure simulacrum."

Baudrillard labels the corresponding orders:

> "In the first case, the image is a good appearance: the representation is of the order of sacrament. In the second, it is an evil appearance: of the order of malefice. In the third, it plays at being an appearance: it is of the order of sorcery. In the fourth, it is no longer in the order of appearance at all, but of simulation."

### The Decisive Turning Point

> "The transition from signs which dissimulate something to signs which dissimulate that there is nothing, marks the decisive turning point."

### The Hyperreal

Opening line of "The Precession of Simulacra":

> "The simulacrum is never that which conceals the truth -- it is the truth which conceals that there is none. The simulacrum is true."
> -- Attributed to Ecclesiastes (Baudrillard's own fabrication/ironic epigraph)

And the definition:

> "It is the generation by models of a real without origin or reality: a hyperreal."

## 4. The Borges Fable and the Precession of Simulacra

**The Borges story:** Jorge Luis Borges, "On Exactitude in Science" (Spanish: "Del rigor en la ciencia"). First published March 1946 in *Los Anales de Buenos Aires* under the joint pseudonym "B. Lynch Davis" (Borges and Adolfo Bioy Casares). Collected in the 1946 second Argentine edition of *A Universal History of Infamy* (*Historia universal de la infamia*). The story is presented as a fictional quotation from "Suarez Miranda, *Viajes de varones prudentes*, Libro IV, Cap. XLV, Lerida, 1658."

**Baudrillard's use of the fable** (from "The Precession of Simulacra," opening pages):

> "If we were able to take as the finest allegory of simulation the Borges tale where the cartographers of the Empire draw up a map so detailed that it ends up exactly covering the territory (but where, with the decline of the Empire this map becomes frayed and finally ruined, a few shreds still discernible in the deserts -- the metaphysical beauty of this ruined abstraction, bearing witness to an imperial pride and rotting like a carcass, returning to the substance of the soil, rather as an aging double ends up being confused with the real thing), this fable would then have come full circle for us, and now has nothing but the discrete charm of second-order simulacra."

**The reversal -- map precedes territory:**

> "The territory no longer precedes the map, nor survives it. Henceforth, it is the map that precedes the territory -- PRECESSION OF SIMULACRA -- it is the map that engenders the territory..."

### On Simulation Becoming Autonomous

> "Simulation is infinitely more dangerous since it always suggests, over and above its object, that law and order themselves might really be nothing more than a simulation."

## 5. Dead Internet Theory -- Current Data

### 5a. Bot Traffic: Imperva/Thales 2025 Bad Bot Report

**Source:** Imperva (a Thales company), "2025 Bad Bot Report." Published April 15, 2025. Data covers calendar year 2024. Based on analysis of 13 trillion bad bot requests across thousands of domains.

**Key finding:** For the first time in a decade, automated traffic surpassed human activity, accounting for **51% of all web traffic** in 2024.

- Bad bot traffic specifically: **37%** of all internet traffic (up from 32% in 2023; sixth consecutive year of growth).
- Good bots (search crawlers, etc.) account for the remaining ~14% of automated traffic.
- 44% of advanced bot traffic targeted APIs.

**Quote:** "The surge in AI-driven bot creation has serious implications for businesses worldwide. As automated traffic accounts for more than half of all web activity, organizations face heightened risks from bad bots, which are becoming more prolific every day." -- Tim Chang, General Manager of Application Security, Thales.

**Sources:**
- Press release: https://cpl.thalesgroup.com/about-us/newsroom/2025-imperva-bad-bot-report-ai-internet-traffic

### 5b. AI-Generated Web Content

**Three complementary studies:**

#### Graphite / CommonCrawl Study

**Source:** "More Articles Are Now Created by AI Than Humans," Graphite, published May 7, 2025 (updated May 8, 2025).

- Analyzed 65,000 English-language URLs randomly selected from CommonCrawl (articles with schema markup, 100+ words, published January 2020 to May 2025).
- AI detection via Surfer's AI detector (500-word chunks; article classified as AI-generated if >50% predicted as AI).
- **Finding:** In November 2024, AI-generated articles surpassed human-written articles for the first time.
- **Quote:** "The quantity of AI-generated articles has surpassed the quantity of human-written articles being published on the web."
- **Plateau:** "The proportion of AI-generated articles has remained relatively stable over the last 12 months" (since roughly May 2024).

Source: https://graphite.io/five-percent/more-articles-are-now-created-by-ai-than-humans

#### Ahrefs 900K Pages Study

**Source:** "74% of New Webpages Include AI Content (Study of 900k Pages)," Ahrefs blog, 2025.

- Analyzed 900,000 newly created English-language web pages detected by Ahrefs' crawler in April 2025 (one page per domain).
- Used in-house AI content detector ("bot_or_not").
- **Findings:**
  - **74.2%** of newly published pages contained AI-generated content.
  - Only **2.5%** were "pure AI" (nearly all content generated without human editing).
  - **25.8%** classified as purely human-written.
  - **71.7%** were a human/AI mix.
- **Note:** 87% of 879 surveyed content marketers admitted to using AI to create or assist content.

Source: https://ahrefs.com/blog/what-percentage-of-new-content-is-ai-generated/

#### Originality.ai Ongoing Study (Google Search Results)

**Source:** "Amount of AI Content in Google Search Results -- Ongoing Study," Originality.ai (January 2019 to September 2025).

- Methodology: 10,000 websites per two-month period, 500 informational keywords, top 20 results per keyword.
- Detection threshold: Originality.ai score of 0.5 or above.
- **Trend data:**
  - February 2019: 2.27%
  - December 2023: 8.48%
  - January 2025: 19.10% (peak at time)
  - July 2025: 19.56% (all-time high)
  - September 2025: 17.31% (most recent)

Source: https://originality.ai/ai-content-in-google-search-results

#### Ahrefs: AI Overviews Citing AI Content

**Source:** "AI Overviews Cite AI-Generated Content More Than Human Writing," Ahrefs blog, 2025.

- **Finding:** 91.4% of content cited in Google's AI Overviews is at least partially AI-generated.
- This represents the feedback loop: AI search features preferentially surface AI-generated content, which then becomes training data and source material for further AI generation.

Source: https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-cite-ai-generated-content-more-than-human-writing/

### 5c. AI Content Citing AI Content: The Closed Loop

#### "Vibe Citing" -- GPTZero / NeurIPS 2025

**Source:** GPTZero, "GPTZero finds 100 new hallucinations in NeurIPS 2025 accepted papers." Published January 21, 2026. Authors: Nazar Shmatko, Alex Adam, Paul Esau (with contributions from Alex Cui and Edward Tian).

- Analyzed **4,841 of 5,290 papers** accepted by NeurIPS 2025.
- Found **100 confirmed hallucinated citations** across **53 papers**.
- GPTZero's Head of ML Alex Adams coined the term **"vibe citing"**: citations that "look accurate at first glance, but crumble under closer inspection." LLMs "derive or amalgamate real sources into uncanny imitations" -- fabricated author names, invented titles, incorrect publication details.
- **Examples:** A citation crediting "John Doe and Jane Smith" as WebVoyager authors with a fabricated arXiv ID. Papers with DOIs leading nowhere. Incomplete arXiv IDs like "arXiv:2305.XXXX."
- **The loop:** These hallucinated citations in peer-reviewed papers become training data for next-generation models, propagating fabricated references through every system trained on that data.

Source: https://gptzero.me/news/neurips/

## 7. AI-Generated Music on Spotify

### The "Fake Artist" / Functional Music Phenomenon

- **Perfect Fit Content (PFC) program:** Spotify launched a program in 2017 to commission cheap music attributed to fake artists and labels for mood/genre playlists (chill, ambient, sleep, piano). Internal sources describe a network of production companies creating tracks for hundreds of fabricated artist names. ([Wikipedia: Controversy over fake artists on Spotify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controversy_over_fake_artists_on_Spotify))
- **Scale:** Swedish newspaper *Dagens Nyheter* discovered in 2022 that approximately 20 musicians had produced tracks for over 500 fabricated names on Spotify. They identified **830 fake artists total**, with **495 placed on Spotify's curated playlists**. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controversy_over_fake_artists_on_Spotify))
- **Streams:** Researchers found that **50 fake artists had a cumulative total of over 520 million streams**. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controversy_over_fake_artists_on_Spotify))
- These pre-date generative AI -- they were cheaply produced human-made functional music. But they established the template that AI generation later scaled massively.

### Spotify's Crackdown on AI-Generated Tracks

- **September 25, 2025:** Spotify announced it had removed **75 million+ "spammy" tracks** in the preceding 12 months. The catalogue of actual music stands at ~100 million tracks, meaning the purge was equivalent to ~43% of the real catalogue. ([Music Business Worldwide, Sept 2025](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-has-deleted-75m-spammy-tracks-as-it-unveils-new-ai-music-policies/))
- Three new policies announced: (1) impersonation enforcement (banning unauthorized AI voice clones/deepfakes), (2) spam filtering system targeting mass uploads/duplicates/SEO hacks/artificially short tracks, (3) AI disclosure standard via DDEX. ([Spotify Newsroom, Sept 25, 2025](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-09-25/spotify-strengthens-ai-protections/))
- Quote -- Sam Duboff, Spotify Global Head of Marketing and Policy: "Our new spam filter will flag tracks and uploaders using these tactics and stop recommending them."
- Quote -- Charlie Hellman, VP Global Head of Music Product: "We're not here to punish artists for using AI authentically and responsibly."
- Financial context: Spotify's total music payouts grew from $1 billion in 2014 to **$10 billion in 2024**, creating massive incentives for spam/fraud.

### The Boomy Situation

- **Boomy** is an AI music startup (founded 2019) whose users had created **14.4 million tracks** to date as of May 2023. ([Music Business Worldwide, May 2023](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/ai-music-app-boomy-spotify-stream-manipulation/))
- **May 2023:** Spotify pulled **tens of thousands of Boomy tracks** (~7% of Boomy's Spotify uploads) after Universal Music Group alerted Spotify to suspicious "artificial streaming" (bot listening) on Boomy tracks. ([Entrepreneur, May 2023](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/spotify-removes-thousands-of-boomys-ai-generated-songs/451985))
- Key nuance: the removal was for **stream manipulation** (bots), not for being AI-generated per se. Spotify later reinstated many tracks after review. ([Mixmag](https://mixmag.net/read/spotify-reinstates-songs-from-ai-music-generator-after-fake-stream-allegations-tech))

### Percentage of New Uploads That Are AI-Generated

- **Deezer data** (the only platform publicly reporting this via its proprietary AI detection tool):
  - January 2025: **10%** of new uploads fully AI-generated
  - April 16, 2025: **18%** -- over **20,000 fully AI-generated tracks per day** ([Deezer Newsroom, April 2025](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2025/04/deezer-reveals-18-of-all-new-music-uploaded-to-streaming-is-fully-ai-generated/))
  - September 2025: **28%** -- over **30,000 tracks/day** ([Deezer Newsroom, Sept 2025](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2025/09/28-fully-ai-generated-music/))
  - November/December 2025: **34%** -- **50,000 AI songs/day** ([Medianama, Nov 2025](https://www.medianama.com/2025/11/223-ai-music-34-daily-uploads-deezer-50k-ai-songs/))
- Critical finding: despite 28-34% of uploads being AI, **AI-generated music accounts for only ~0.5% of total streams**, and **70% of that 0.5% was identified as fraudulent streaming**. ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/50000-ai-tracks-flood-deezer-daily-as-study-shows-97-of-listeners-cant-tell-the-difference-between-human-made-vs-fully-ai-generated-music/))
- Quote -- Aurelien Herault, Deezer Chief Innovation Officer: "AI generated content continues to flood streaming platforms like Deezer, and we see no sign of it slowing down."
- Deezer's detection tool identifies content from major generative models including **Suno** and **Udio**.
- [UNVERIFIED] Spotify and Apple Music have not publicly disclosed equivalent percentages.

## 8. Trevor Paglen's "Adversarially Evolved Hallucinations" (2017-2018)

### The Project

- Part of the exhibition **"A Study of Invisible Images"** at **Metro Pictures, New York**, September 8 -- October 21, 2017. ([Metro Pictures](https://www.metropictures.com/exhibitions/trevor-paglen4))
- Opening reception September 8, 2017; artist walks September 9 and 23; discussion with AI researcher **Kate Crawford** on September 16.
- Paglen created images using a two-network adversarial system: a **Discriminator** trained on unconventional datasets, and a **Generator** that produced images to fool the Discriminator. After thousands/millions of iterations, the Generator learned to produce images of things it had never seen.
- Instead of training on standard object categories (kitchen utensils, cars), Paglen created custom training sets based on **literature, folklore, philosophy, and psychoanalysis**: "Interpretation of Dreams" (Freudian symbols), "Omens and Portents" (eclipses, comets), "American Predators" (eagles, stealth bombers), vampires, post-human landscapes.
- The resulting images are entirely synthetic -- visualizations of what paired AIs collectively "imagined."
- The exhibition addressed three kinds of invisible images: **training libraries, machine-readable landscapes, and images made by computers for themselves**.
- The work emerged from years of collaboration with software developers and computer scientists, including a residency at Stanford University.

### Key Quotes from Paglen

- **"Something dramatic has happened to the world of images: they have become detached from human eyes. Our machines have learned to see without us...I call this world of machine-to-machine image-making 'invisible images.'"** ([LensCulture](https://www.lensculture.com/articles/trevor-paglen-an-urgent-look-at-how-artificial-intelligence-will-see-the-world))
- **"These are images that were not made for human eyes. They are made for machine eyes."** (on the facial recognition portraits) ([LensCulture](https://www.lensculture.com/articles/trevor-paglen-an-urgent-look-at-how-artificial-intelligence-will-see-the-world))
- **"What does it mean that 'seeing' no longer requires a human 'seer' in the loop?"** ([LensCulture](https://www.lensculture.com/articles/trevor-paglen-an-urgent-look-at-how-artificial-intelligence-will-see-the-world))
- **"If we want to understand the invisible world of machine-machine visual culture, we need to unlearn how to see like humans."** ([Brooklyn Rail, Oct 2017](https://brooklynrail.org/2017/10/artseen/TREVOR-PAGLEN-A-Study-of-Invisible-Things/))
- **"The invisible world of images isn't simply an alternative taxonomy of visuality. It is an active, cunning, exercise of power..."** ([Brooklyn Rail](https://brooklynrail.org/2017/10/artseen/TREVOR-PAGLEN-A-Study-of-Invisible-Things/))
- Paglen considers this development in machine vision **"more significant than the invention of photography."** ([Brooklyn Rail](https://brooklynrail.org/2017/10/artseen/TREVOR-PAGLEN-A-Study-of-Invisible-Things/))

### Critical Reception

- Mixed. The show lacked wall text, relying on essays and walkthroughs. Critic Sadie Rebecca Starnes noted the "gleaming prints are laconic, cold and easily dismissed" on first encounter but require "patience and curiosity."
- Jerry Saltz dismissed the work as "Conceptual Zombie Formalism" rooted in "smarty-pants jargon."
- More sympathetic critics (Brooklyn Rail, LensCulture, Hyperallergic) treated it as urgent and prescient work about algorithmic power.
- Paglen is a **MacArthur Fellow** and winner of the **Electronic Frontier Foundation's Pioneer Award**.
- A book, *Trevor Paglen: Adversarially Evolved Hallucinations*, was published by Sternberg Press. ([Sternberg Press](https://www.sternberg-press.com/product/trevor-paglen/))

## 9. AI Training on AI Output -- Model Collapse

### Shumailov et al. (2024) -- the Nature Paper

- **Title:** "AI models collapse when trained on recursively generated data"
- **Authors:** Ilia Shumailov, Zakhar Shumaylov, Yiren Zhao, Nicolas Papernot, Ross Anderson, Yarin Gal
- **Institutions:** University of Oxford, Cambridge, Imperial College London, University of Toronto, University of Edinburgh
- **Published:** *Nature*, volume 631, pages 755-759, **July 2024**. DOI: 10.1038/s41586-024-07566-y. ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07566-y))
- **Key findings:**
  - Indiscriminate use of model-generated content in training causes **irreversible defects** in resulting models.
  - **Tails of the original content distribution disappear** -- rare/minority outputs are progressively lost.
  - Errors compound across generations: each generation reproduces previous errors and introduces new ones.
  - After recursive training, language models **produce probable sentences too frequently** and generate **meaningless word sequences that no human would produce**.
  - The effect occurs across model types: **LLMs, variational autoencoders, and Gaussian mixture models**.
  - They termed this **"model collapse"** -- a degenerative process where models progressively lose the ability to generate diverse, high-quality output.
  - **Quote:** Models "start misperceiving reality based on errors introduced by their ancestors."
  - **Practical implication:** "The value of data collected about genuine human interactions with systems will be increasingly valuable in the presence of LLM-generated content in data crawled from the Internet."
  - **Memorable example:** Text about medieval architecture, by the ninth recursive generation, devolved into a list of jackrabbits.

### Other Research on AI Training on AI Data

- **Model Autophagy Disorder (MAD):** Researchers drew an analogy to **mad cow disease** -- AI systems consuming their own outputs leads to progressive quality and diversity degradation. Referenced by Sadowski. ([Twitter/X](https://x.com/jathansadowski/status/1678921415486222337))
- **Visual degradation findings:** When AI headshot generators were fed their own outputs recursively, researchers observed a **"bizarre convergence of facial features."** ([France24, Aug 2024](https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20240805-inbred-gibberish-or-just-mad-warnings-rise-about-ai-models))
- **Text degradation:** By the **fourth iteration** of recursive training, one model was "spouting complete gibberish" when asked to complete sentences. ([France24](https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20240805-inbred-gibberish-or-just-mad-warnings-rise-about-ai-models))
- A recurring **yellow filter** in AI-generated images was attributed to the homogenization effect of recursive AI training, not just the popularity of "Ghibli-style" images. ([Worldcrunch](https://worldcrunch.com/tech-science/ai-inbreeding-the-phenomenon-threatening-artificial-intelligence/))

### "Habsburg AI" -- Jathan Sadowski

- **Coined:** February 13, 2023, on Twitter/X by **Jathan Sadowski** (sociologist, Monash University). ([Tweet](https://x.com/jathansadowski/status/1625245803211272194))
- **Definition (exact quote):** "Habsburg AI -- a system that is so heavily trained on the outputs of other generative AI's that it becomes an inbred mutant, likely with exaggerated, grotesque features. It joins the lineage of Potemkin AI."
- The term was first used on the podcast **Machine Kills** (co-hosted by Sadowski).
- Sadowski later noted the Shumailov et al. Nature paper as validation: "The Habsburg AI theory is yet again validated...Training large-language models on data created by other models...causes 'irreversible defects in the resulting models.'" ([Tweet, July 2024](https://x.com/jathansadowski/status/1817690404675879242))
- Also see: Cory Doctorow's related framing as **"Coprophagic AI"** -- AI eating its own waste. ([Doctorow, Medium, March 2024](https://doctorow.medium.com/https-pluralistic-net-2024-03-14-14-inhuman-centipede-enshittibottification-8608fa181e57))

## 10. Streaming and Temporal Flattening

### Catalogue Music vs. New Releases -- Market Share Trends

- **Ted Gioia, "Is Old Music Killing New Music?"** -- published January 2022 on *The Honest Broker* (Substack). Widely cited. ([The Honest Broker](https://www.honest-broker.com/p/is-old-music-killing-new-music))
- Key data points (citing MRC Data, now Luminate):
  - **Old songs represent 70% of the US music market** (as of 2021 MRC Data).
  - The **200 most popular new tracks account for less than 5% of total streams**, down from roughly double that three years prior.
  - **All growth in the market is coming from old songs.** New music consumption is shrinking.
  - Record labels are prioritizing **acquisition of publishing catalogs** from aging artists over investing in new talent.
- **Ted Gioia followup:** "In the Music Business, 80 Is the New 20" -- the 80/20 rule has inverted, with old music dominating. ([The Honest Broker](https://www.honest-broker.com/p/in-the-music-business-80-is-the-new))

### Luminate Data (Updated Figures)

- Catalogue's share of US music consumption: **69.8% in 2021 -> 72.2% in 2022 -> 72.6% in 2023**. ([Luminate](https://luminatedata.com/blog/a-look-at-trends-in-catalog-streaming/))
- US On-Demand Audio Catalog streaming: **73.57%** through H1 2023. (Luminate defines "catalog" as releases more than 18 months old.)
- **Over 90%** of Jazz, Holiday/Seasonal, New Age, and Blues streaming is catalog; **89.77%** of Rock streaming is catalog.
- **Deep catalog** (5+ years old) now accounts for roughly **half the market**.
- When analyzing the Top 500k US tracks in On-Demand Audio streaming (end of 2022): 51.3% from tracks released 2018-2022. By 2023: tracks released 2019-2023 make up **48.3%** of US audio streaming in the Top 500k -- i.e., the majority of even "recent" listening skews to tracks already several years old.

### Grammy Awards as Proxy for New-Music Cultural Relevance

- Viewership fell from **~40 million** a decade ago to **18.7 million in 2020**, then crashed **53% year-over-year** to **8.8 million in 2021** -- the lowest on record.

### Playlists Mixing Decades / Temporal Flattening

- No single definitive academic study found on "temporal flattening" of playlists per se. However:
- Spotify's own research and product design confirms that algorithmic playlists draw from a user's **entire listening history**, mixing eras freely. Each playlist "reflects not only what you love today, but the full arc of your taste." ([Spotify Engineering, 2023](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2023/04/humans-machines-a-look-behind-spotifys-algotorial-playlists))
- **The Decision Lab** analysis: Spotify's algorithm "behaves more like a conservative radio station, prioritizing listener retention over novelty," favoring familiar tracks from any era.
